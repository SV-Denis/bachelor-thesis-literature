1. Ce problema..., 

Applying EARS to a Requirements Engineering and User Experience (RE + UX) course

for first-year Software Engineering (SE) Bachelor students.

Testing EARS requirement transformation to a trainee-level public



2. Cum se rezolva?, 

Brief lectures on EARS 

3. Exemple/use-cases, 

the students were asked to take four of the requirements they had already produced, either SRS-style or as user stories, and rewrite them using EARS patterns. Afterwards they provided relevant data about their EARS utilization compared to their former requirements (benefits, drawbacks, difficulties,etc)

results: Mixed opinions from students, the EARS section was difficult to mark as the majority of students couldnt clearly show which original requirements were converted to EARS

4/26 groups of students found EARS redundant to SRS requirements, found it hard to structure text, found it too technical or had difficulties indentifying what patterns to use
4/26 found it that it makes requirements easier to understand
13/26 had mixed feedback

4. Open questions?

How should we structure courses/labs to improve the opinion of students in regards to EARS
Should novices be forced to cover every EARS pattern, and how should EARS handle non-functional requirements

Side note: it could tie well with why we should be trying to automatize the transformation of requirements into EARS
